Sampling variation and the central limit theorem

We draw random samples of size n from a made-up population and compute each sample's average Ȳ. Different samples give different averages. What does the distribution of all these averages look like?

How to use this simulation
  1. Keep Annual earnings and n = 30. Click Draw 1 sample several times. Each sample (blue dots) gives a different average Ȳ (orange line). This is sampling variation.
  2. Click Draw 1,000. The bottom chart collects all the Ȳ's: it shows the sampling distribution of the sample average.
  3. Choose n = 1 and draw 1,000: the histogram looks just like the population. Then try n = 5, 30 and 500, drawing 1,000 each time. The shape turns into a bell curve.
  4. Tick Show the normal curve and click Zoom in to compare the histogram with the normal distribution the CLT predicts.
  5. Repeat with the other populations: the die (flat) and employed (only zeros and ones).

Key idea: whatever the population looks like, the average of a large sample is approximately normally distributed around μ, with spread σ/√n. This is the central limit theorem.
