Statistics and OLS: interactive simulations

These simulations go with the recap lecture. They follow the lecture's running example: education and earnings in the CPS, and our Stata regression reg log_w_wage year_ed (N = 165, β̂ = 0.046, SE = 0.017). Each page opens with step-by-step instructions and the key idea to take away.

The law of large numbers

Draw people one at a time from a skewed earnings population and watch the sample average settle on the population mean. With five researchers, every path is different, but all of them get there.

Lecture topic: Law of large numbers 2

Find the best line

Drag a line through 40 men's education and earnings and try to make the sum of squared residuals as small as possible. Then reveal the OLS line and compare.

Lecture topic: How does OLS find the line? 2b

Regression and the CEF

Five steps: a regression with only a constant gives the mean; the average at one education level by hand vs by regression; checking both against the true CEF when it is straight or curved; and a saturated model (low vs high education) that always recovers the CEF.

Lecture topic: Three reasons to love OLS 3

Sampling variation and the CLT

Every sample has a different average. Draw 1,000 samples at n = 1, 5, 30 and 500: the histogram of averages turns into a bell curve with spread σ/√n, whatever the population looks like.

Lecture topic: The central limit theorem 3b

One sample, one slope

Draw a new sample of men and run the regression again. Same population, same regression, but a different β̂ every time.

Lecture topic: Sampling variation 4

The sampling distribution of β̂

Repeat the regression on 1,000 samples. The β̂'s form a normal curve centred on β, with spread equal to the standard error. Sliders show what makes the SE smaller.

Lecture topic: β̂ ~ N(β, SE²) 5

t-statistics, p-values and power

With the true β = 0, about 5% of studies are "significant", and all of them are false positives. Load our Stata result to see t = 2.71 and p = 0.007 as shaded tails.

Lecture topic: Hypothesis testing 6

What "95% confident" means

100 studies, 100 intervals, one fixed truth. About 95 of them contain the true β. Our Stata interval [0.012, 0.079] is shown on top.

Lecture topic: Confidence intervals

Tips

  • Everything runs in your browser, with nothing to install. A laptop or tablet works best.
  • The populations are simulated, so, unlike in real research, we know the true values (μ and β) and can check how close the estimates get.
  • You can't break anything: reload the page at any time to start again.
  • The numbers change every time you run a simulation. That is the point: they come from random samples.
