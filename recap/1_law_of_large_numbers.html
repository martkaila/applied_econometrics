The law of large numbers

The top chart is a made-up population: the annual earnings of 20,000 people. Like real earnings data, it is skewed, and 15% of people earn nothing. Because we built it, we know its true mean μ = E[Y]. In real life we only see a sample. How close does the sample average Ȳ get to μ?

How to use this simulation
  1. Click Draw 1 person a few times. Each click adds one randomly chosen person (blue arrow in the top chart) and updates the average Ȳ on the right. With only a few people, Ȳ jumps around a lot.
  2. Click +10 and +100. In the bottom chart, the blue line is Ȳ as the sample grows. Its swings get smaller.
  3. Click Run to n = 10,000. Ȳ settles on the red dashed line, μ. The blue outline in the top chart (the sample) also takes the shape of the population.
  4. Switch to 5 researchers and run again. Each researcher gets a different sample and a different path, but all five end up at μ.
  5. Tick Show the LLN funnel. About 95% of sample averages fall inside the shaded band, and the band narrows as n grows.

Key idea: with a large enough random sample, the sample average Ȳ gets as close as we like to the population mean E[Y]. This is the law of large numbers.
