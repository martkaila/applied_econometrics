Econometrics · Recap · Simulation 5

Hypothesis testing: from one t-statistic to a p-value

We test H₀: β = 0 (schooling has no effect on wages) in three steps. 1. Our one sample gives one t = β̂ / SE. 2. Imagine H₀ is true and draw many samples: how is t spread? 3. The p-value is the share of those t's at least as far from 0 as ours.

How to use this simulation
  1. Step 1. Click Draw a sample (or Our Stata result). Like a real researcher, you get one β̂, one SE and one t, marked in orange below.
  2. Step 2. Click Draw 1,000. Each sample comes from an imagined world where β = 0. The histogram of their t's is the sampling distribution of t if H₀ is true.
  3. Step 3. Click Compute the p-value. Red bars: imagined t's at least as far from 0 as ours. Their share is the p-value. The black curve is the theory shortcut (the CLT), which gives almost the same number.
  4. Click Researcher's view. Step 2 disappears, because in real life we have one sample. The curve stays, and that is why one sample is enough for a test.
  5. Extra: open False positives and power to run many studies in the world you chose. With true β = 0, about 5% reject H₀ (false positives). With β = 0.046, the rejection rate is the power.

Key idea: the p-value is the probability of a t-statistic at least as extreme as ours if H₀ were true. It is not the probability that H₀ is true.

Same picture twice: dividing each β̂ by its own standard error turns the left histogram into the right one. That is all a t-statistic is.
