Econometrics · Recap · Simulation 5

Hypothesis testing: t-statistics, p-values and power

We test H₀: β = 0 (schooling has no effect on wages) with t = β̂ / SE, and reject H₀ when |t| is larger than the critical value (≈ 1.96). Here you choose the true β, so you always know whether a "significant" result is a real discovery or a false positive.

How to use this simulation
  1. Keep true β = 0, so H₀ is true. Click Run 1 study: a sample of men is drawn, the regression is run, and you see its β̂, SE, t and p-value on the right.
  2. Click Run 1,000. About 5% of the studies reject H₀. All of them are false positives. That is what a 5% significance level means.
  3. Move true β to 0.046 and run 1,000 again. Now H₀ is false, and the rejection rate is the test's power: its chance of detecting the effect. Then try a larger N.
  4. Click Load our Stata result. Our t = 2.71 is compared with the distribution of t if H₀ were true. The shaded tails are the p-value.
  5. After running a study, click Researcher's view to hide everything a real researcher cannot see: the population and all the other studies.

Key idea: the p-value is the probability of a t-statistic at least as extreme as ours if H₀ were true. It is not the probability that H₀ is true.
