Econometrics · Recap · Simulation 2

Find the best line

A sample of 40 men: years of education (horizontal) and annual earnings in thousands of dollars (vertical). Which straight line fits them best? OLS picks the line that makes the sum of squared residuals as small as possible. Can you find it by hand?

How to use this simulation
  1. Go through the four steps above the chart with Next step (or click a step): draw the data, fit a line, show the residuals, square the residuals.
  2. Move the blue line. Drag its middle to shift it up or down, or drag near one of its ends to tilt it. The two sliders on the right do the same.
  3. In step 3, each red segment is a residual: actual minus predicted earnings. Try to make their plain sum zero: many bad lines manage it, because positive and negative residuals cancel.
  4. In step 4, each orange square is a residual squared. Make their total area, the sum of squared residuals, as small as you can. The chart at the bottom shows where your line (orange dot) sits on the "bowl" of possible lines.
  5. Click Reveal the OLS line to see the best possible line, and New sample to try again with 40 different men.

Key idea: OLS chooses the intercept and slope that minimise the sum of squared residuals, Σ(Yi − α − βXi)².

The minimisation problem, live

The black curve is the smallest sum of squared residuals you can get with each slope, if the intercept is also chosen as well as possible. The orange dot is your current line; it sits above the curve whenever your intercept is off. OLS finds the bottom of this bowl: arg minα,β Σi (Yi − α − βXi)².

Earnings are in thousands of dollars ($k). The data are simulated to look like the CPS.
