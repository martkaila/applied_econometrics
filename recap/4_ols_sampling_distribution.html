The sampling distribution of the OLS estimate β̂

The grey cloud is a made-up population of men: years of education and log weekly wage, with a true return to schooling of β = 0.046 (4.6% per year). Each sample of men gives its own OLS estimate β̂. Here we draw many samples and look at the distribution of all the β̂'s. The starting settings match our Stata example: N = 165, Root MSE σ = 0.64, SD of education 2.9.

How to use this simulation
  1. Click Draw 1 sample a few times. Each sample gives a new line and a new β̂, which drops into the histogram at the bottom.
  2. Click Draw 1,000. The β̂'s form a bell curve centred on the true β.
  3. Tick Show the CLT curve. Compare three numbers: the SD of the β̂'s across all samples, the SE formula, and the SE Stata would print from one sample. They agree.
  4. Move one slider at a time and draw 1,000 again: sample size N, noise σ and SD of education. Which changes make the SE smaller?
  5. Hover over a bar of the histogram to highlight those regression lines in the scatter plot.

Key idea: β̂ ~ N(β, SE²) with SE = σ / √(N × Var(X)). More data, less noise or more variation in X give a more precise estimate.
